Setup Guide

A quick walkthrough to get your Trading Journal Pro up and running in Notion — under 5 minutes, zero config.

01

Get your duplication link

After checkout on Lemon Squeezy you'll receive an email with a Notion duplication link. Check your inbox (and spam) — it should arrive instantly. Haven't received it? Email jayvinn.pacheco@gmail.com.

02

Duplicate into your workspace

Click the duplication link. Notion opens a preview — hit Duplicate in the top-right. The full journal copies into your personal (or team) workspace as a private copy — no shared-editing conflicts.

03

Tour the layout

Three main views: Trade Entry (add trades), Dashboard (hit-rate / R:R / biggest wins & losses), and Daily / Weekly / Monthly P&L roll-ups. All pull from the same underlying database — data stays in sync everywhere.

04

Start logging trades

The Trade Entry database is ready for new rows — no template work needed. Each field maps cleanly: Ticker, Setup Type, Entry / Exit, Risk ($), Outcome ($ / R:R), Date, Notes. Just open the entry view and fill it in.

05

Set your risk parameters

Before logging real trades, confirm your risk-per-trade in the Risk field is consistent. R:R calculations depend on it. Pick a percentage (most traders use 1% or 2%) and stick with it — journal integrity lives and dies by accurate risk data.

06

Verify the dashboard

After logging at least one trade, open the Dashboard view. Win-rate, average R:R, and your best / worst sessions should appear automatically. Nothing showing? Check that trades have the correct date and the dashboard filters haven't been silently narrowed.

07

Attach chart screenshots

Use Notion's embed block or image upload inside each trade row for entry and exit charts. Being able to revisit your screenshot context later is one of the journal's biggest advantages — you'll spot patterns you missed while live.

08

Archive, don't delete

Once a trade is closed and reviewed, archive the row — don't delete it. Archiving keeps it queryable in the dashboard roll-ups without cluttering your active entry list. Right-click a row in the database → Archive.

09

Schedule a weekly review

Set a recurring Friday close reminder. During your review: spot check win-rate by setup, compare daily P&L streaks, and flag any emotional or structural patterns. The journal rewards habit — the numbers only move if you look at them.
